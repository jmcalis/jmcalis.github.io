Classical models of collective behavior often assume that emergent dynamics result from external and observable interactions among individuals. However, how collective dynamics in human populations depend on the internal psychological processes of individuals remains underexplored. Here, we develop a mathematical model to investigate the effects of internal psychology on dynamics of collective action. Our model is grounded in the theory of planned behavior—a well-established conceptual framework in social psychology that links intrinsic beliefs to behavior. By incorporating temporal biases in social perception and individual differences in decision-making processes, we find that the interplay between internal and external drivers of behavior can produce diverse outcomes, ranging from partial participation in action-taking to rapid or delayed behavioral cascades. Predicting these outcomes is, in general, challenging because distinct outcomes can be preceded by transient dynamics that are qualitatively similar to one another. Nevertheless, in the case of two individuals, we derive a complete, analytical classification of long-term outcomes into three mutually exclusive categories. Our model provides a useful test bed for methods that aim to predict the emergence of collective action, and it lays the groundwork for studying the nuanced dynamics of collective human behavior arising from the interaction between psychological processes and observable actions.
